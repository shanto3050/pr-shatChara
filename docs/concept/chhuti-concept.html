Game concept · draft 1 · 30 October 2026

ছুটি Chhuti

Fourteen days of summer holiday at Nanabari. Play Sat Chara, cricket and ha-du-du, steal mangoes at noon, and get home before the maghrib azan.

Genre nostalgic open-village adventure with folk-game matches Platform Android first Engine Unity (URP) Working title Chhuti (ছুটি, "holiday")

The pitch

One line: the street games you grew up with, set in the village you remember, over one summer holiday you never wanted to end.

Shuvo is twelve and lives in a Dhaka flat. When school closes for the summer he is sent to Nanabari, his grandparents' village of Hijoltoli, for two weeks. His cousins and the neighbourhood kids have a problem. The end-of-holiday tournament against Uttar Para is in fourteen days, last year they lost badly, and their team has fallen apart: the captain has quit, one boy works on the river every afternoon, one girl isn't allowed on the field, and one is banned for breaking a window.

Shuvo is a city kid. He can't swim, he has never flown a kite, and he doesn't know the rules of Sat Chara. Over fourteen days he learns the village, brings the team back together one friend at a time, and finds out that his quiet Nana was once the best Sat Chara player the village had ever seen.

What kind of game this is (and isn't)

The idea started as "GTA in rural Bangladesh": an open place to wander with missions in it. The open village and the missions stay. The crime goes. The closest models are:

ReferenceWhat we take from it
Boku no Natsuyasumi (My Summer Vacation, Japan)The whole frame: a child's summer at relatives' house in the countryside, days with a rhythm, nostalgia as the main feeling. It proves the genre works.
Bully (Rockstar)A small open world seen at a kid's level, missions built from everyday trouble, friendships and rivals, a schedule you are supposed to follow and can break.
Yakuza seriesDense small districts packed with real-life minigames that are fun on their own.
Stardew ValleyA calendar, people with daily routines, relationships that grow through small favours.
Real Cricket 24The graphics benchmark for the matches, and proof that Bangladeshi players buy into realistic local sport.

Why people will play

  • To install it: nostalgia. Almost every Bangladeshi adult has these memories, and nobody has made this game. It's the kind of thing people share on Facebook with "this was my childhood".
  • To keep playing: the matches. Sat Chara's rebuild-under-fire round, tape-ball cricket and ha-du-du are tense, short and skill-based. Each one has to be fun on its own, even without the story.
  • To finish it: the people. You want to get Sajib back as captain, see Nusrat play, beat Tipu's team and hear the end of Nana's story.
  • To come back after finishing: playing Sat Chara and cricket online with friends, in the village they now know.

Design pillars

Every feature has to serve at least one of these. If it serves none, it's cut.

It feels like being there

The light at 4 pm, the sound of the azan across the fields, the noon heat, the smell you can almost see in the steam off the rice. Realistic graphics serve this pillar, not the other way round.

Play is the core

Folk games are the main verbs, not side content. Each one has real rules, real skill, and is worth playing again.

Small stakes, big feelings

Nobody dies, nothing explodes. The stakes are a lost ball, a scolding, a friend who won't talk to you, a match you want to win. That's what childhood stakes were.

Warm and respectful

Elders, religion, poverty and village life are shown the way people remember them: with affection and humour, never mockery.

Setting and era

Hijoltoli হিজলতলী

A fictional village in central Bangladesh, named after the hijol trees along its khal. It's about an hour by launch and van from the district town. A river on one side, rice fields on the other, a bazaar where the two roads meet. It's fictional so we can put everything iconic in one walkable place, and so no real village is singled out.

The village is split into paras (neighbourhoods). Shuvo's grandparents live in Dokkhin Para (south). The rivals are Uttar Para (north). There's also Pal Para, where the potter families live, and Ghat Para by the river, where the fishing families live.

Season: Jaistha, the honey month জ্যৈষ্ঠ · মধুমাস

Mid-May to mid-June. Schools close for the summer holiday. It's mango, lychee and jackfruit season, the afternoons are hot and long, and kalboishakhi storms come in late in the day. Every storm ends with children running out to collect the mangoes it knocked down.

Era: around 2001

Recommended: the summer after Bangladesh became a Test-playing nation (June 2000). It's the peak nostalgia window for today's 25–40-year-olds, and it removes smartphones from the story:

  • One television in the village, at Shiuli's father's tea stall: black-and-white, with the antenna on a bamboo pole. Everyone crowds in for cricket and the Friday film.
  • Cricket fever is everywhere. Every kid wants to bowl like Shoaib Akhtar and has a tape-ball with insulation tape wound tight.
  • Letters and cassettes from relatives working abroad come by the postman's bicycle.
  • Load-shedding every evening, hurricane lanterns and hand fans.
  • Landline at the bazaar phone shop only. Getting a call from Dhaka is an event.
Real cricketers, songs, TV shows and brands are not used by name or likeness. That avoids licensing problems. We reference the feeling (a famous win on the radio, "the Friday film"), not the property.

The village map

One village, about 400 × 400 m, walkable end to end in two to three minutes. Small enough to fill with detail and to run on mid-range phones; big enough that the far orchard feels like an adventure. Everything is reachable on foot; Mama's van-rickshaw is a shortcut later on.

A day in the holiday

Each day runs on the village clock. A full day takes about 20–25 minutes of play, which fits a phone session. Time flows while you roam and stops in conversations, cutscenes and matches (a match takes a fixed slice of the afternoon). Prayer times give the day its shape, as they do in a real village.

05:00–08:00BhorFajr azan, roosters. Fishing with Nana or Bablu, maktab for Tuni, panta bhat or muri for breakfast.
08:00–12:30SokalErrands and missions: bazaar, orchard work, helping Nani. Free roam.
12:30–15:30DupurBath in the pond, lunch, then Nani's enforced nap. Sneak out: noon is mango-stealing time.
15:30–18:15BikelAsr azan. The best time: matches, games, practice, the team.
18:15–19:00SondhaMaghrib azan: everyone runs home. Wash feet, lamps lit.
19:00–22:00RaatStudy, load-shedding, Nani's stories, fireflies, the TV at the bazaar on some nights. Sleep saves the day.

Rules of the day

  • Maghrib curfew. When the azan starts you have about two real minutes to get home. Late means Nani waiting at the gate, a scolding scene and an extra chore tomorrow. Never a game over.
  • Noon nap. Nani makes everyone lie down. You can wait out the clock or sneak out (a short stealth sequence past the sleeping house). Getting caught costs you nothing but face.
  • Energy and hunger are light: you get slower and your aim worse if you skip meals. Eat at home (free) or buy snacks (ice cream, chanachur, jhalmuri).
  • Nights are short. One or two things at most, then sleep.

Main story

Structure

Fourteen days in four chapters. Chapter 1 is a full Sat Chara game in a small part of the village and can ship on its own.

ChapterDaysWhat it's aboutNew places and games
1. Arrival1–3Shuvo arrives, meets the family and cousins, learns Sat Chara, loses badly to Uttar Para in a friendly. Learns about the tournament.Nanabari, pond, the para courtyards. Sat Chara, kanamachi, marbles.
2. Gathering the team4–7Win back Bablu, Joy, Shiuli and Nusrat. The first storm. The tablighi visit. The haat.Bazaar, orchard, river. Cricket, fishing, carrom, mango collecting.
3. The captain8–11Sajib comes back. Nana's secret comes out. Kite day on the char. The first real win against Uttar Para (in cricket).Kheya ghat, char, bamboo grove at night. Kites, ha-du-du, swimming race.
4. The tournament12–14A cousin's wedding, the last practice, the final, the goodbye.Wedding courtyard, the full school field. Danguli, the final Sat Chara match.

The ending

The tournament final is Sat Chara, best of five rounds, on the school field with the whole village watching. Nana walks out before the last round and gives Shuvo his seven old river stones to use as the tower. Win or lose, the team celebrates; the game gives a slightly different scene for each, and both are good endings.

On day 14 the family walks Shuvo to the launch ghat. The team runs along the bank as the launch pulls out. Tuni throws him something: a marble, the one he lost on day 2.

Epilogue: twenty years later

Grown-up Shuvo comes back with his own child. The orchard is smaller, the bazaar has a mobile-phone shop and a concrete mosque, the field has a fence. Nana's verandah is empty. Under the hijol tree, a group of kids are playing Sat Chara with a stack of flat stones. Shuvo's child asks, "Baba, how do you play this?" The game hands you the controls one last time. This scene is the emotional payoff and the trailer's last shot.

Day-by-day outline

Main beats only. Every day also has free time for side quests and games. Tap a day to open it.

Day 1The launch ghatTue · haat day
  • Opening: the launch arriving at the ghat in the morning haze; Mama waiting with the van. Credits over the van ride through the rice fields (tutorial: looking around).
  • Nani at the gate, Nana on the verandah, cousins Mitu and Tuni. Explore the house and courtyard (movement tutorial).
  • Noon: Mitu shows the pond ghat. Shuvo can't swim, which is a running joke and a later goal.
  • Afternoon: kids playing Sat Chara in the uthan. Mitu teaches the rules: knock the tower, then rebuild while the others try to hit you (Sat Chara tutorial).
  • Maghrib: the first azan; everyone scatters home. Curfew tutorial.
  • Night: load-shedding, hurricane lamp, Nani's first story.
Day 2Uttar Para
  • Morning: marbles with Joy and Tuni. Shuvo loses his best marble to Tipu.
  • Meet the rest of the para kids. Learn about the end-of-holiday tournament run by the youth club, and last year's defeat.
  • Afternoon: a friendly Sat Chara match against Uttar Para. Shuvo's team loses badly (the game is tuned so a loss is likely; winning gives a bonus scene).
  • Night: Mitu says it plainly: "We don't have a team any more." Shuvo makes a list of who's missing. This list is the main quest log.
Day 3Tuni is too small
  • Kanamachi and gollachut in the courtyard with the younger kids; Tuni can't be caught.
  • Rana's sprained ankle; Nani's remedy. He'll heal by day 8 and coaches running meanwhile.
  • Shuvo argues Tuni into the team. Chapter 1 ends: the first four kids together.
Day 4Bablu's river
  • Before dawn: go out on the river with Bablu and his father. Net fishing minigame.
  • Finish the catch early, sell it at the bazaar. Bablu's father agrees to let him play in the afternoons.
  • Afternoon: first cricket match at the school field. The cow on the pitch.
Day 5Kalboishakhi
  • Joy's ball through Haji saheb's window (flashback cutscene). Apologise together; Haji saheb puts both boys to work in the orchard for three mornings.
  • Late afternoon: the sky goes dark green, wind, the first kalboishakhi storm. Run home. Then the best scene in the game: everyone running out into the wet orchard collecting fallen mangoes (aam kurano), timed collection race against Tipu's gang.
  • Night: mango feast on the verandah.
Day 6FridayFri · jumma
  • Morning: bath, new panjabi, Nana takes Shuvo to jumma prayer. A short, quiet, non-interactive scene; meeting half the village outside after prayer.
  • Afternoon: the tablighi brothers on their gasht stop at the field before asr and invite the boys to the mosque. Play continues or the boys go; both are fine (see Life interrupts).
  • Evening: the Friday film on the tea-stall TV. The whole bazaar crowded in.
Day 7Shiuli's shift, Nusrat's mother
  • Cover Shiuli's tea-stall shift: tea-making and serving minigame, eavesdrop on village gossip (story hints).
  • Nusrat's mother: Nani visits with a box of pitha and a long chat. Nusrat joins as "adviser".
  • End of chapter 2: seven of eight. The captain is still missing.
Day 8The carrom board
  • Sajib at the youth club. Challenge him at carrom (best of three). Win, and he listens.
  • He says he'll come back if Shuvo can swim across the pond: "A team can't carry someone who can't even swim." Swimming lessons begin, with Bablu.
Day 9Nana's trunk
  • Noon: looking for a cricket bat in the storeroom, Shuvo finds Nana's tin trunk: an old photo of a boys' team, a dented trophy, and seven smooth river stones.
  • Night: Nana tells the story of the 1960s match (a cutscene with grainy, sepia visuals). He teaches one old trick: how to stack under pressure.
Day 10Across the pond
  • Shuvo swims across the pond. Sajib returns as captain: the midpoint of the story. The full team practises together for the first time.
  • Ha-du-du practice match against the Ghat Para teenagers.
  • Night: fireflies by the bamboo grove; the courage walk past the grove after Nani's story.
Day 11Kite day on the char
  • Make a kite with Joy (paper, bamboo strips, rice glue); coat the string with glass manja.
  • Rahman majhi rows everyone to the char. Kite fight against Uttar Para.
  • Cricket rematch against Uttar Para. The first win. Tipu is rattled.
Day 12The wedding
  • A cousin's wedding: gaye holud in the courtyard, turmeric on everyone's faces, decorating the gate with banana stems and marigolds, running errands for the cooks.
  • Night: the whole village at the feast. Shuvo and Tipu end up sitting together.
Day 13The last practice
  • Morning danguli; afternoon full-team Sat Chara practice with Nana watching from the edge of the field.
  • Ammu calls the bazaar phone: Shuvo goes home the day after tomorrow.
  • Night: the team on the pond ghat, feet in the water, talking about next summer.
Day 14The final
  • Morning: the youth club marks the field with lime powder. Everyone dresses up a bit.
  • Afternoon: the tournament final, Sat Chara, best of five rounds. Nana's stones for the last round.
  • After asr: celebration (or a consolation scene). The next morning: the launch, the team on the bank, Tuni's marble. Then the epilogue.

Folk games

The core of the game. Each game is built so it works as a standalone mode from the main menu once you've played it in the story. The three marked core get full production (realistic animation, AI teammates, online play). The medium ones get solid but simpler builds. The rest are short, one-screen minigames.

Side quests

About 30 small stories, each 5–15 minutes, picked up by talking to people. They reward coins, collectables, friendship and stat points.

  • The ball in the pond. Dive for it before the match (after swimming is learned; before that, persuade Bablu).
  • Kite in the tree. Climb the jackfruit tree to get Joy's kite.
  • Tiffin carrier. Take Mama's lunch to the bazaar before it gets cold.
  • The lost goat. Find a neighbour's goat by following the sound of its bell.
  • A real bat. Save coins to buy a willow-look bat from the bazaar. Replaces the frond stem.
  • Tape for the ball. Buy insulation tape and wind your own tape-ball.
  • Pitha morning. Help Nani make bhapa pitha (timing minigame).
  • Fish for dinner. Catch three fish for Nani.
  • The letter from abroad. Read Bablu's uncle's letter aloud for his grandmother, who can't read.
  • The radio. Find batteries for Nana's radio before the big match broadcast.
  • The bioscope. Pay a coin to look through the bioscope on haat day.
  • Buy a kite. Bargain with the kite seller.
  • Marigolds. Collect flowers for the wedding gate.
  • Gopal kaka's wheel. Try making a clay pot; it collapses (comic).
  • Bhulu the dog. Feed the stray every day; he follows you and later finds lost things.
  • The hidden marble. Win back the marble Tipu took on day 2.
  • Maktab morning. Walk Tuni to the maktab; wait outside, chat with the Imam.
  • Matchbox labels. Collect all 20 village matchbox labels.
  • The cow on the pitch. Gently herd the cow off the field before the match.
  • Haji saheb's mornings. Three mornings in the orchard: chase off crows, gather fallen mangoes, fix the fence.
  • The van ride. Ride with Mama to the haat; help unload.
  • Firefly jar. Catch fireflies, then let them go (Nani's rule).
  • The bamboo grove. Walk past it at night to prove you're not scared.
  • Phone call. Wait at the phone shop for Ammu's call.
  • The photo. Find out who the other boys in Nana's old team photo were; two still live in the village.
  • Straw drying. Help spread the boro straw before the rain comes.
  • Hawai mithai. Spend your first coin on candy floss.
  • Boat race. Cheer and help Ghat Para's boat team prepare.
  • The broken window. Buy glass from the bazaar with saved coins.
  • Tipu's leather ball. It goes into the khal; get it back for him, and make a friend.

Life interrupts

A system, not just story beats: the village happens around you and sometimes stops your game. These are the moments people remember and the ones that make the world feel real. Each event has a probability, a time window and a reaction from the kids.

EventWhenWhat happens
Maghrib azanEvery eveningThe match ends at once. Kids grab their sandals and run in every direction. Curfew starts.
Asr azan during playAfternoonsOlder boys pause and some leave for the mosque; the game continues with fewer players or waits, the kids decide (a short vote).
Tablighi gashtDays 5–7, afternoonsThree or four of the visiting brothers walk up to the field, greet the boys warmly and invite them to the mosque for asr and a short talk. Game systems

Progression

  • Shuvo's stats (speed, aim, power, agility, plus swimming, climbing and stamina) rise through practice and life skills. The four match stats start at 10 points and cap at 14.
  • Friendship (0–5 per kid). Raised by helping them, playing with them, small gifts (a kite, an ice cream). At 3 you unlock their personal story; at 5, a team move in matches (e.g. Mitu's covering throw).
  • Village standing. How the adults see you. High standing opens doors: Haji saheb lets you into the orchard, Monu mia lets you watch TV for free.
  • Mischief. Stealing mangoes, sneaking out at noon, climbing where you shouldn't. Fun, and the kids like you for it; but if an adult sees you, word reaches Nani by evening (a gossip system with a delay) and you get a scolding and a chore. Never violence, never failure.

Money and items

  • Coins and small notes (taka) from errands, selling fish, helping at stalls, and Nana on the last day. Prices are era-appropriate (to be checked with people who were kids then).
  • Spend on: snacks, ice cream, kites and string, tape, a tennis ball, the bat, gifts, the bioscope, glass for the window.
  • Equipment: bat (frond stem → plank → bought bat), ball (rag ball → tape-ball → Tipu's leather ball as a gift), kite, borshi, marbles, latim.

Collections

Marbles (with names and patterns), matchbox labels, kites, stamps from letters, bird sightings (doel, kingfisher, koel, myna, egret), fish species caught, Nani's stories heard. A "memory book" stores the photos Shuvo takes with a borrowed film camera on special days, which double as the gallery for the ending.

Matches

  • Pick the team from the kids who are available today (who's ill, at work, called home).
  • AI teammates follow the captain's calls once Sajib is back.
  • Difficulty tunes itself gently in the story; the standalone and online modes are fixed and fair.

Save and time

  • Auto-save at each phase of the day; full save at night with Nani.
  • Missed something? Most side quests stay open for a few days; nothing in the main story can be missed. After the ending, "one more summer" mode lets you roam the village freely with every game unlocked.

Culture and respect

This game will be played by the people it depicts, and the reaction in Bangladesh will make or break it. These are rules, not suggestions.

Religion

  • Azan, prayer, maktab, the Imam and the tablighi visit are part of village life and are shown warmly and accurately. They're never a joke, a villain or an obstacle the game wants you to resent.
  • Prayer itself is never a minigame and never shown in close interactive detail. Scenes cut to before and after.
  • Going to the mosque is always a free choice with no stat reward or penalty. The game doesn't preach in either direction.
  • Recorded azan by a real muezzin, with permission. Quran recitation is not used as background music.
  • The Pal Para potter family is Hindu and shown as neighbours like any others: Gopal kaka's courtyard, a small shrine (tulsi plant) in their yard, a sweet from their kitchen. No interfaith conflict storylines.

People

  • Poverty is present (Joy's father pulls a rickshaw, Bablu works) but nobody is pitied or mocked for it.
  • Girls play, lead and win. The real-life friction (Nusrat's mother, Mami) is shown honestly and resolved with family warmth, not lectures.
  • No mockery of disability, mental illness, skin colour or dialect. Nicknames stay kind (no "Kalu").
  • Adults scold; they don't hit. A teacher's cane can appear on a wall, never in use.

Children's safety

  • Swimming is learned with an older kid present; the game shows why (Nani's warnings, Ammu's call). No drowning content.
  • Kite manja is shown, with Nani warning about cut fingers; no injuries shown.
  • No smoking or tobacco items, even as collectables (cigarette pack collecting was real; matchbox labels replace it).

Politics

No party symbols, no hartal storylines, no real politicians. The "chairman" is a family relation, not a political figure.

Review step: before each chapter ships, three or four reviewers from different backgrounds (one religious scholar, one from a village family, one woman who grew up in a village, one from a Hindu family) play it and sign off on these points.

Art and sound

Visual direction

  • Realistic, with Real Cricket 24 as the bar for the matches. Believable proportions, real materials, natural movement.
  • The light is the star. Hazy dawn, hard white noon, the golden afternoon (most of the play happens here), purple dusk with smoke from the clay stoves, lantern nights. Lighting presets per phase, baked where possible for phone performance.
  • Season colours: ripe yellow-green mangoes, dry gold straw, deep green banana and bamboo, red-brown mud walls, the blue-grey of a coming storm.
  • Built so far (in this repo): the Nanabari courtyard props (mud house, haystack, tube well, banana and coconut palm, kolshi, jute sack, clothes line, straw drying, pond, van-rickshaw, fence), the Bablu character mockup, and motion-captured actions (throws, pick-ups, duck, dodges, walk and jog). All of it carries straight into chapter 1.
  • Reference trip: a weekend in a real village with a phone: photos for textures and proportions, video for motion, audio for the soundscape. This is the single most valuable art task.

Sound

  • Field recordings (free, and better than any library): azan from the village mosque at distance, roosters, the koel calling in the heat, doel song at dawn, frogs after rain, crickets at night, the tube well handle, pond splashes, the haat crowd, a bicycle bell, the ice cream bell, a launch horn.
  • Voices: standard Bangla with a light regional colour. Child voice actors for the kids (with guardians present, properly paid), older actors for Nani and Nana. Short barks for matches ("দৌড়া!", "ধর!").
  • Music: original score with bamboo flute (banshi), ektara and dotara, light percussion. Quiet in free roam, livelier in matches, solo flute for the ending. No licensed songs.

Controls and tech

Controls

  • Left thumb: virtual joystick. Right thumb: one context button (talk, pick up, climb, throw) plus a run toggle.
  • Each game has its own gesture set (see Folk games), introduced by a kid teaching you, never by a text wall.
  • Camera follows behind; matches use a broadcast camera like the existing Sat Chara build.

Audience and business

Who it's for

  • Main audience: Bangladeshi adults aged 18–40, in the country and abroad, who grew up with these games or heard about them from their parents. They have mid-range phones or better and buy games occasionally.
  • Second: teenagers who want a local game that isn't a battle royale, and play the online folk-game modes with friends.
  • Third: players outside Bangladesh who like slow, warm games (the Boku no Natsuyasumi and Stardew audience), reached through English subtitles and festivals.
  • Expected rating: Everyone / PEGI 7.

Making money

Recommended: chapter 1 free, the rest a one-time unlock, plus optional cosmetics for online play (kite designs, lungi and jersey colours, marble skins). No ads during the story, no loot boxes, no pay-to-win, no energy timers. Price to be tested; check local payment options (carrier billing, mobile wallets) because card ownership in Bangladesh is low.

Chapter 1 free does two jobs: it's the marketing (the nostalgia hits in the first ten minutes) and it tests whether people want more before the expensive chapters are built.

This is several times the size of Sat Chara alone. It's only realistic if it's built in shippable slices, with each slice tested on real players before the next.
